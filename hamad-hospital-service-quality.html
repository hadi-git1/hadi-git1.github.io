Case Study — Marketing Research

Hamad Hospital Service Quality Assessment

A 33-question patient satisfaction survey assessing clinical care, accommodation, food, and parking at Hamad Hospital — including how COVID-19 disrupted service delivery and patient communication.

  • Team 3-Person Group Project
  • Course Marketing Research — MAKT 300
  • Year 2021
  • Focus Survey Design & Data Analysis

Overview

Hamad Hospital is one of Qatar's largest government-funded hospitals. Our goal was to assess inpatient satisfaction across clinical care, non-clinical service, accommodation, food, parking, and auxiliary facilities — and to understand how the COVID-19 pandemic had changed the patient experience. We designed and deployed a 33-question online survey, then analyzed the results in Excel to identify concrete, costed recommendations for the hospital.

Methodology

Using convenience sampling, we distributed the survey through Reddit, Qatar Living, and WhatsApp groups, gathering 32 respondents — filtered down to the 21 who had actually stayed overnight at the hospital via a screening question. Question design followed strict principles from class: no double-barreled or leading questions, ambiguous wording avoided, and sensitive COVID-19 questions placed at the end of the survey. Results were analyzed in Excel using descriptive statistics (percentages, averages, standard deviations) on the nominal/ordinal data.

On the positive side, doctor communication, pharmacy service, and overall accommodation cleanliness (3.86/5) scored well. The clearest weak points were reception wait times, food freshness, and the near-total absence of shops/library facilities for patients and visitors.

COVID-19 Impact

Only 4 of the 21 overnight patients in our sample stayed during the pandemic, but their responses were telling: communication with staff dropped sharply under social-distancing protocols, and satisfaction with food and auxiliary services fell further as non-essential facilities closed. On the hospital's side, we noted the standup of new COVID-specific departments, expanded ambulance services, and dedicated communication centers — a reasonable emergency response, even if the patient-facing experience suffered in the short term.

Recommendations

  • Reception staffing: add 4–5 receptionists (~QR 3,500/month each) and split reception by purpose (appointments, urgent help, visits) to reduce overcrowding.
  • Food quality: hire 1–2 additional experienced chefs (QR 3,000–5,000/month) to improve freshness and menu variety.
  • Parking system: introduce an appointment-priority parking app (~QR 200,000, 3–5 month build) plus reserved spaces for urgent-care patients.
  • Patient library: a small dedicated reading room (~QR 10,000 setup) to give patients and visitors a low-cost, high-value amenity.
